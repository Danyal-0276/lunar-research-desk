ALDERBRIDGE UNIVERSITY
UNDERGRADUATE STUDENT HANDBOOK
2026–2027 Academic Year | Edition 1.0
Effective 1 August 2026
Fictional university and policies for the Handbook Navigator project

01. Scope and interpretation
This handbook applies to undergraduate students at Alderbridge University, including students enrolled part time and online. Programme supplements may specify additional academic requirements. The Registrar publishes term dates and individual deadlines in the student portal. Week-based deadlines in this handbook apply to standard 15-week terms. For shorter courses, use the dates shown in the course schedule. Unless a policy specifies calendar days, business days exclude weekends and university holidays. Deadlines end at 5 p.m. campus local time unless the academic calendar gives a different time.

02. Admission and enrolment
New students must accept their offer by the date in the admission letter, provide any outstanding records, and complete required advising before registering. Students must keep their name, contact details, and emergency contact current in the student portal. Registration is confirmed when a course appears as enrolled in the portal. Waitlisted students may attend with instructor permission while waiting for a seat. Attendance alone does not establish enrolment.

03. Registration and study load
A standard undergraduate study load is 12 to 18 credits per term. Students seeking more than 18 credits must obtain written approval from their academic adviser and programme director before registering. Students must meet published prerequisites or receive a written waiver from the department offering the course. Students considering a reduced load should consult Financial Aid and, where applicable, International Student Services before changing their schedule.

04. Course additions and drops
Students may add or drop courses through the end of week two using the student portal. An added course is subject to seat availability and prerequisites. An on-time drop does not appear on the transcript. Tuition adjustments follow the separate refund schedule in Section 13. Students should save their portal confirmation because a conversation with an instructor or adviser does not change registration.

05. Course withdrawal
After the drop deadline, students may withdraw from a course through the end of week eight. Submit a course withdrawal request in the student portal. An approved withdrawal appears as W on the transcript, earns zero credit, and is excluded from the GPA. After week eight, students may petition the Registrar for a late withdrawal based on documented exceptional circumstances. A late withdrawal requires approval. Until the portal confirms a withdrawal, the student remains enrolled and responsible for course requirements.

06. Leave of absence and university withdrawal
A student planning to pause their studies should request a leave of absence through the Registrar. An approved leave normally covers one or two consecutive standard terms. The approval notice states the return term and any conditions. Students withdrawing from every course should submit a university withdrawal request. The Registrar confirms the effective date, and Student Accounts determines any tuition adjustment under Section 13. Students returning after their approved leave should contact the Registrar before registration opens.

07. Attendance and missed coursework
Instructors publish attendance and participation requirements in the course syllabus. Students who expect to miss a class or assessment should contact the instructor as early as possible. If advance notice is impractical, students should contact the instructor as soon as they are able. Instructors may arrange a makeup assessment, an extension, or an equivalent assignment when appropriate. An approved accommodation follows Section 15 and the terms in the student's accommodation letter.

08. Grades and grade point average
The university uses the following grade points per credit.
A = 4.0, A− = 3.7, B+ = 3.3, B = 3.0, B− = 2.7, C+ = 2.3, C = 2.0, C− = 1.7, D+ = 1.3, D = 1.0, F = 0.0.
The GPA equals the sum of grade points multiplied by course credits, divided by the total graded credits attempted. W (withdrawal), I (incomplete), and pass/fail marks are excluded from the GPA. A passing mark in a pass/fail course earns credit. A programme may require a grade above D for a course to satisfy a specific requirement.

09. Incomplete grades and grade review
An instructor may assign an Incomplete when a student has completed most of the course and exceptional circumstances prevent timely completion. The instructor and student document the outstanding work, completion deadline, and grade that will apply if the work is not submitted. A student who believes a final grade contains a calculation error or departs from published grading criteria should contact the instructor within ten business days of grade posting. If unresolved, the student may submit a written review request to the department chair within ten business days of receiving the instructor's response.

10. Academic standing and progression
Undergraduate students are in good academic standing when their cumulative GPA is at least 2.0. A student below 2.0 enters academic probation and must meet with an adviser before registering for the next term. The adviser and student prepare a written recovery plan. If the cumulative GPA remains below 2.0 after two consecutive enrolled terms on probation, the Academic Standing Committee reviews the record and issues a written decision about continued enrolment. The decision includes any applicable appeal deadline.

11. Academic integrity
Students must submit their own work, acknowledge sources, and follow assessment rules for collaboration, outside assistance, and permitted tools. Course instructions specify whether generative AI may be used and how it must be disclosed. Students should ask the instructor before using an AI tool when instructions are unclear. When a suspected violation is referred, the university gives the student written notice of the allegation and an opportunity to respond. The decision letter states the finding, any sanction, and the appeal process.

12. Transfer credit and graduation
The Registrar evaluates transfer credit using official transcripts and, when requested, course descriptions or syllabi. A transfer award specifies whether the credit applies to a particular requirement or counts as an elective. To graduate, students must complete the credit, curriculum, and residency requirements for their programme and hold a cumulative GPA of at least 2.0. Students must submit a graduation application by the academic calendar deadline. The Registrar reviews the degree audit and confirms eligibility after final grades are posted.

13. Tuition, fees, and refunds
Student Accounts posts tuition, fees, payment deadlines, and the refund schedule in the student portal. Refund eligibility depends on the effective date of a confirmed registration change and the schedule for the term. A drop or withdrawal can affect financial aid, scholarships, and the balance due. Students may request a review of an account charge through Student Accounts and should include the transaction date and relevant confirmation. Housing and meal plan refunds follow the applicable agreements.

14. Financial aid and scholarships
Financial Aid publishes eligibility and renewal requirements for each award. Students should review their award notice before changing credit load, taking leave, or withdrawing. The office may adjust an award after an enrolment change or a review of academic progress. Students with exceptional circumstances may request a review and submit supporting documentation by the deadline in their notice.

15. Accessibility and accommodations
Students may request academic accommodations through Accessibility Services at any time. The office reviews requests individually and issues an accommodation letter when approved. Students should share the letter with instructors and discuss arrangements early enough for implementation. Disability documentation is submitted to Accessibility Services. Requests for accessible course materials, exam arrangements, or campus access should be directed to that office.

16. Library and technology
During standard teaching weeks, the main library is open Monday through Friday from 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. and Saturday from 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. It is closed on Sundays. The library publishes revised hours for exams, breaks, and holidays. Items must be returned or renewed by the due date shown in the library account. Students must protect their university credentials, use university systems responsibly, and report suspected account compromise to the IT Service Desk.

17. Student conduct and reporting
Students are expected to respect others, comply with reasonable safety instructions, and use university facilities and systems responsibly. Prohibited conduct includes harassment, discrimination, threats, theft, and deliberate damage to property. Students may report conduct concerns to Student Affairs. For an immediate threat or emergency, contact local emergency services and notify Campus Safety when safe to do so. The university communicates applicable procedures and response deadlines when it opens a conduct case.

18. Complaints and appeals
Students should use the process designated for the decision or concern. Grade reviews follow Section 09, and appeals of academic integrity or academic standing decisions follow the instructions in the decision letter. For a general service complaint, contact the responsible office. If the matter remains unresolved, submit a written complaint to Student Affairs with relevant dates, prior correspondence, and the requested resolution.

19. Student records and privacy
Students may request a transcript or enrolment verification through the Registrar. To request a correction to an administrative record, identify the record and provide supporting information. Grade disputes follow Section 09. The university limits access to student records to authorised purposes and handles release requests under its published privacy policy and applicable requirements. Students can review their contact details and consent settings through the student portal.

20. Student support and office directory
Registrar: Registration, enrolment changes, academic records, transcripts, and graduation.
Academic Advising: Course selection, programme requirements, study load, and probation plans.
Student Accounts: Billing, payments, refunds, and account enquiries.
Financial Aid: Awards, scholarship renewal, and enrolment changes affecting aid.
Accessibility Services: Accommodation requests and accessible learning arrangements.
Student Affairs: Student conduct, general complaints, and referrals to support services.
Counselling and Wellness: Confidential appointments and referrals for student wellbeing.
Campus Safety: Campus safety concerns, incident reporting, and emergency coordination.
International Student Services: Support for international students considering enrolment changes.
IT Service Desk: University accounts, learning systems, and technology support.

21. Handbook updates
The Registrar maintains the current handbook and publishes revisions in the student portal with their effective dates. Students with questions about which edition applies to a decision should contact the office responsible for that decision.
